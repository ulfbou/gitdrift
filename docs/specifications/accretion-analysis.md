# Accretion analysis

## Purpose

Accretion analysis describes cumulative semantic evolution across many changes without treating file growth, age, or high churn as inherently defective.

## Subject

The subject may be an artifact, fragment, concept, representation role, fact set, or obligation set. Analysis uses one or more explicit comparison windows.

## Observations

Accretion observations may include transition count, semantic-transition count, mechanical-only transitions, artifact and fragment count, fact additions and removals, representation roles, obligation-state changes, and contradiction lifetime.

These observations select or describe evidence. They do not independently establish architectural quality or regression.

## Semantic accumulation

The analysis records facts, concepts, and roles introduced, modified, duplicated, retired, or left unresolved over the window. It distinguishes cumulative growth from weakening, contradiction, mixed authority, and obsolete residue.

## Cumulative regression

A cumulative-regression candidate requires an identified obligation or coherence relation, evidence across the selected window, counterevidence, and a bounded claim explaining why no single transition alone captures the full condition.

The candidate remains unresolved when authority, lineage, equivalence, or window completeness is insufficient.

## Outputs

An accretion record contains subject, window, raw transition references, semantic deltas, obligation evaluations, descriptive signals, alternatives, counterevidence, completeness, and limitations.

## Constraints

- Large files are not automatically blobs in a negative sense.
- Frequent change is not automatically risk.
- More concepts or roles are not automatically incoherent.
- Mechanical changes remain raw evidence even when suppressed from a semantic projection.
- A final judgment follows the case and judgment-policy contracts.
