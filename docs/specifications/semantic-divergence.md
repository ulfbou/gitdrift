# Semantic divergence

## Purpose

This specification separates graph and artifact difference from semantic change and regression.

## Ordinary divergence

Ordinary divergence exists when selected histories contain different reachable commits or transitions. It is descriptive and does not imply overlap, error, or semantic significance.

## Overlapping change

Overlapping change exists when alternate states modify the same path, artifact identity, fragment, symbol, concept representation, fact, or obligation subject. Overlap selects material for deeper analysis but does not itself establish contradiction or regression.

## Semantic divergence

Semantic divergence exists when alternate states contain meaningfully different facts, representations, authorities, or obligation evaluations for the same relevant subject.

A semantic-divergence record identifies the subject, compared states, facts, correspondence method, supporting evidence, counterevidence, and completeness.

## Semantic regression

Semantic regression is a policy-governed conclusion that an established active obligation became weaker, violated, or contradictory across a specific state comparison or transition without authorized retirement or supersession and with required evidence completeness.

Divergence from the controlling state is not automatically regression. A candidate may be equivalent, stronger, intentionally superseding, or unresolved.

## Convergence

Convergence exists when alternate chains arrive at equivalent relevant facts or obligation satisfaction, whether through shared commits, patch-equivalent changes, or independently supported semantic representations.

Convergence preserves provenance. Equivalent endpoints do not erase distinct histories or transition evidence.

## Unresolved divergence

Divergence remains unresolved when authority, subject correspondence, semantic equivalence, supersession, or evidence completeness is insufficient. Unresolved is a valid classification, not an execution failure.

## Classification invariants

1. Change size and churn are not verdicts.
2. Deletion does not prove semantic loss.
3. Unique commits do not prove unique semantic value.
4. Similar text does not prove equivalence.
5. Branch names do not establish authority.
6. Regression references the controlling obligation and exact comparison or transition.
7. Counterevidence and alternatives remain part of every possible-regression case.
